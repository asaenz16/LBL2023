# Testing plaquette baths and coherent system evolution

The tested modifications do not improve preparation of distant image
correlations from the maximally mixed state. The most reliable comparison
uses exact eight-spin density matrices. Plaquette updates reduce the
distant correlation, coherent evolution at the original bath strength
makes little difference, and weaker baths perform worse. Larger tensor
runs show short correlation ranges, but tiny plaquette signals are
sensitive to truncation and should not be quoted as converged values.

A subsequent [finite-stage timing study](#finite-hamiltonian-then-bath-stages)
finds that alternating H-only and bath-only intervals can outperform
simultaneous evolution with matching exposures. None of its tested
schedules outperforms the bath-only baseline on the eight-spin image.

## Models and comparison protocol

All runs retain the image Hamiltonian, kappa=2, open boundaries, sample
edge-contrast sigma, and spin normalization of `ladder-robustness.md`.
Images have alternating black/white 2x2 blocks on 2xL strips. Effective
inverse temperature b=6 is held fixed; beta_paper=2Lb. There is no added
Pauli noise in this comparison, and all fresh runs start from I/2^(2L).

**Original bond bath:** sum of two-spin Davies generators, as before.

**Modification 1:** each adjacent pair of rungs forms a four-spin
plaquette. Its Hamiltonian includes both vertical bonds and both
horizontal bonds at their original strengths. Resolve local Pauli
couplings using this entire plaquette Hamiltonian. Apply the overlapping
plaquette channels in even-half / odd-full / even-half layers.

For the main comparison, each spin's total Pauli proposal weight equals
its weight in the original bond model. A boundary spin belongs to one
plaquette and two physical bonds, so its proposal weight is 2. An interior
spin belongs to two plaquettes and three bonds, so its weight in each
plaquette is 3/2. These weights multiply dissipator rates, not Hamiltonian
terms or inverse temperature. They match a coupling-strength convention,
not actual mixing times. A separate exact control assigns weight 1 to
every plaquette proposal; it also performs worse than the original bath.

The plaquettes overlap and omit external bonds. Their Gibbs states do not
assemble into the full Gibbs state. In particular, including larger local
neighborhoods is not a theorem that the global stationary state improves.

**Modification 2:** simulate

\[
 \dot\rho=-i[H,\rho]+\eta\sum_e\mathcal L_e(\rho),
 \qquad \eta\in\{1,0.2,0.05\}.
\]

Each bond Davies generator commutes with the commutator generated by its
own bond Hamiltonian. The default `fused` integrator therefore combines
the two local channels exactly and uses spatial Strang splitting of
these combined generators. This includes every physical Hamiltonian bond
with the correct total weight. It reduces tensor contractions compared
with wrapping a complete bath sweep between two Hamiltonian sweeps.
The alternative `separate` integrator was also tested on eight spins;
both approximate the same continuous generator as the step decreases.

Elapsed time t and dissipative exposure u=eta*t are recorded separately.
We compare late-time states after stabilization, not weak baths after an
arbitrarily short common sweep count. Eight-spin convergence is checked
using full-state trace distances between records; larger simulations
check both seed correlation profiles. Neither model guarantees the
global Gibbs fixed point. In modification 2, adding -i[H,rho] cannot
cancel the Gibbs stationarity defect because [H,rho_Gibbs]=0.

## Larger-strip results and numerical qualification

Both modifications were run at 16, 24, and 32 spins with step 0.1 and
maximum tensor bond dimension 32. Plaquettes were rerun at dimension 64;
the 16-spin case has an additional dimension-128 refinement. Coherent
evolution with eta=1 has dimension-64 refinements at 16 and 32 spins.
The weaker eta values were tested exactly at eight spins; they were not
extrapolated as verified large-system results.

Bond-dimension sensitivity is substantial for the plaquette bath. At
16 spins, the distant correlation changes from about 7.58e-4 at dimension
32 to 1.99e-4 at dimension 64 and 1.918e-4 at dimension 128. The last
refinement changes the far signal by about 3.7%, while the original bond
bath gives about 0.00400 at this size. At 32 spins it changes from about 8.01e-8
to 3.09e-9. These tiny values are **not numerically certified**. The data
show why a stabilized trajectory alone does not establish convergence
with respect to tensor truncation.

The coherent eta=1 runs remain close to the original bath in their
correlation scale, with no observed improvement. Exact conclusions about
the small difference at large separation need stronger numerical error
control. The firm negative result is the exact eight-spin comparison;
the larger runs provide exploratory size dependence.

At 32 spins, the dimension-64 coherent refinement ends with far-corner
Gamma about 5.38e-6 and interior-pair Gamma about 2.96e-4, compared with
6.11e-6 and 3.18e-4 for the original bath. Its last five-step profile
change is 3.25e-8, but it exhausts the allocated refinement before three
successive changes pass the stopping rule; `stabilized` remains false.

Energy and long-distance order need separate scoring. At 16 spins and
step 0.1, the dimension-128 plaquette state has excess energy about
0.35253 per spin, below the original bath's 0.36355, while its distant
correlation is about twenty times smaller. Improving energy alone would
therefore give a misleading assessment of this segmentation observable.

See `results/bath-modifications/comparison-summary.json` for final
profiles, energy, physical time, exposure, residuals, and truncation
diagnostics. Its `stabilized` field describes the convergence criterion
actually met. A refinement that exhausts its step budget is not silently
declared converged. The density MPS is not globally positivity-preserving.
We record the Hermiticity defect and the smallest eigenvalue of the
Hermitian part of adjacent reduced operators. At 32 spins and plaquette
dimension 64, the largest per-step trace correction is 0.00221 and the
largest two-rung Hermiticity defect is 0.000118 in Frobenius norm. These
are additional reasons not to interpret its 3e-9 far signal quantitatively.

The full Python suite passes 61 tests. New checks compare the plaquette
Hamiltonian with independent Pauli embeddings, verify channel positivity
and trace/Gibbs preservation locally, check proposal weighting, compare
complex-state dense and MPS updates, verify coherent step convergence
against the full Hamiltonian, and check the fused local Lindbladian
exponential directly.

## Quantum circuit resource assessment

We compiled an explicit thermal-collision realization for each type of
local bath. Each frequency-resolved contact uses k system qubits plus one
resettable thermal qubit: k=2 for a bond, k=4 for a plaquette. The bath is
prepared thermally with an unread measurement and refreshed after contact.
There is no postselection. Compilation uses Qiskit `u3,cx`, optimization
level 1, seed 1200, and no coupling-map routing. Counts sum independently
compiled contacts and can be reduced by specialized synthesis.

| Local template | Contacts/cycle | CNOTs/cycle | Resets/cycle |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Same-color bond | 12 | 202 | 24 |
| Cross-color bond | 12 | 203 | 24 |
| Same-color plaquette | 92 | 37,420 | 184 |
| Boundary-spanning plaquette | 160 | 67,680 | 320 |

Each contact also uses one bath-preparation measurement in this encoding.
Classically sampling the bath's initial energy state can replace that
measurement. Optimizing consecutive resets can reduce their count.

The contrast template uses the 2x4 coupling and the plaquette template
uses interior weights 3/2. Larger-size couplings, endpoint weights, and
half-step angles may change synthesis constants. The following ladder
counts are **template-based estimates**, not a routed full-circuit result.

For even L there are 4L same-color and L-2 cross-color bond-channel calls
per Strang sweep. The plaquette sweep has L same-color calls and L/2-1
boundary-spanning calls. At L=16 (32 system spins), this gives:

| Protocol | CNOT estimate per unsubdivided collision sweep |
|---|---:|
| Original bond bath | 15,770 |
| Plaquette bath | 1,072,480 |
| Bond bath + coherent evolution | 15,770 + 234 |

For coherent evolution, each two-spin XXZ unitary is synthesized with
3 CNOTs. The fused split has 5L-2 such blocks per sweep, giving the 234
additional CNOTs at L=16. These gates do not add ancillas. Compilation
and circuit resource records are in `circuit-resources.json`.

### Matching a circuit to the simulated channel

The simulations exponentiate each local Lindblad generator. A finite
sequence of individual thermal collisions is a different local channel
at nonzero contact step; smaller contact steps are needed to approximate
the simulated channel. The table above does not include that subdivision.

We calibrated a local update of duration 0.1 on maximally mixed,
computational-basis and seeded random pure states. With 64 collision
subdivisions, the largest sampled trace errors are about 4.81e-4 for
the bond templates and 7.05e-4 for the plaquette templates. These sampled
errors are not worst-case channel bounds and do not certify a whole
preparation circuit. Using 64 subdivisions multiplies the bath CNOT
estimates by approximately 64: about 1.01 million for a 32-spin bond
sweep versus 68.6 million for a plaquette sweep. This is an illustrative
reference implementation, not a lower bound or a selected hardware design.

An exact finite channel can alternatively be implemented via a general
Stinespring dilation. A k-spin channel may have Kraus rank up to 4^k,
requiring up to 2k environment qubits. This trades repeated single-ancilla
contacts for a larger local isometry; it has not been compiled here.

### What scales, and what remains unproved

At fixed cluster size and local precision, both constructions need O(n)
local channel calls per sweep for n system spins. Local preprocessing
uses constant-size matrices; no full-system diagonalization is needed for
the dynamics. The exact global Gibbs calculation is only a small-system
validation tool. Increasing the cluster size itself incurs exponential
local matrix and generic synthesis costs.

One bath qubit can be reused serially, giving n+1 qubits for the collision
implementation before routing. Parallel execution of disjoint clusters
uses O(n) bath qubits and a constant number of colored spatial layers,
with a large local contact depth. Ancilla connectivity and reset latency
need an actual device-specific schedule. The tensor bond dimension is a
classical simulation parameter, not a hardware qubit requirement.

Linear work per sweep is not an end-to-end efficiency guarantee. The
number of sweeps, contact subdivisions needed for a global error target,
state-preparation repetitions, and hardware errors still matter. At fixed
physical step, reducing eta requires more elapsed time and more steps
to reach comparable dissipative exposure. Moreover, estimating a tiny
correlation sign by independent Z measurements generally requires shots
proportional to the inverse square of its magnitude. Rapid convergence
to a state with negligible distant correlations is not successful
segmentation preparation.

These numerical modifications are also distinct from retaining full
system evolution during a physically filtered system-bath interaction.
That alternative is analyzed in
[Ding et al., 2508.05703](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05703v3).
Its model-specific guarantees are not inherited by adding H to our
existing bond dissipator. A full-H KMS-filtered construction with its
required coherent correction is another route to investigate:
[Ding, Li and Lin, 2404.05998](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05998v5).

## Finite Hamiltonian-then-bath stages

This experiment tests a different dynamical protocol, not a different
numerical integrator for modification 2. Let C(rho)=-i[H,rho] and let
D=sum_e L_e be the same original edge-local Davies bath. One cycle is

\[
 \rho_{m+1}=e^{\tau_B D}\left(
 e^{-i\tau_H H}\rho_m e^{i\tau_H H}\right).
\]

The full H acts during the first interval and only D acts during the
second interval. The image Hamiltonian still defines the bath jump
operators and rates; these are not rederived with H set to zero. This is
a switched, engineered-channel model, not a demonstration that switching
off a physical Hamiltonian leaves its passive thermal bath unchanged.
Implementing it requires controlling the coherent term during bath
contacts, or implementing the bath channels digitally.

For comparison, the simultaneous cycle is exp(tau_H C + tau_B D).
It matches both integrated generator strengths. At the same cycle duration
T=tau_H+tau_B this corresponds to continuous generator
(tau_H/T) C + (tau_B/T) D. Alternatively, for tau_H>0 it is H at full
strength with eta=tau_B/tau_H for time tau_H; that convention has a
different wall-clock duration. Correlation-versus-bath-exposure plots use
u=m tau_B. Equal cycle counts alone do not give equal bath exposure.

Parameters are unchanged: 2x4 alternating black/white blocks, kappa=2,
sample edge-contrast sigma, b=6, no added noise, initially I/256. Times
are dimensionless in the existing exchange/rate units, with hbar=1.
The first H interval leaves I/256 unchanged. Subsequent unitary intervals
can redistribute correlations, but conserve total H energy and the
spectrum/entropy of the density matrix.

### What was run

The main grid has 35 pairs:

- tau_H = 0, 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 4;
- tau_B = 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2.

There are 19 simultaneous ratio controls, including every ratio in that
grid, and two shorter-period controls at fixed tau_H/tau_B=80. In total,
56 trajectories were saved. Five representative late-time cycles were
also sampled at 101 and then 201 points per stage.

`sequential_bath.py` builds the full eight-spin sum D as a sparse
Liouvillian and applies its exponential with SciPy `expm_multiply`.
The H-only stage uses a full-H spectral unitary. Simultaneous controls
exponentiate tau_H C+tau_B D directly. Thus there is no spatial or H/bath
Trotter splitting error in this reference, and no tensor truncation.
An invariant zero-magnetization-difference operator sector reduces the
vector dimension from 65536 to 12870. The generator's leakage out of this
sector is explicitly checked and was zero numerically. Density matrices
remain full 256x256 states when measured.

Convergence requires three consecutive one-cycle changes with
sqrt(256)/2 times the Frobenius norm below 1e-9, after at least three units
of bath exposure. This upper-bounds the full-state trace distance. Every
saved trajectory passed its stopping rule. Final directly computed
one-cycle trace-distance residuals were below 1.8e-10. This tests
convergence from I/256, not uniqueness of the cycle for every initial
state. Maximum H-stage energy drift was 1.1e-14; final trace errors were
below 6.4e-13 and Hermiticity errors below 1.8e-14. All final states had
positive eigenvalues (minimum across sequential runs about 1.77e-5).

### Measurements within the cycle

The state approaches a periodic cycle. It need not become stationary
within the H-only or bath-only stages. For tau_H=4, tau_B=0.05, Gamma
changes from 0.062591 to 0.058248 during H evolution and returns during
the bath stage. Its physical-time cycle average is 0.060582, still about
30.5% above the matched simultaneous value 0.046414. All sampled points
in this cycle are above that simultaneous control, so this example is
not solely an after-bath sampling advantage.

For (4,1), the after-bath correlation is 0.080509, the minimum sampled
correlation is 0.073399, and the cycle average is 0.077339. The simultaneous
value 0.076163 lies within that oscillation range. Measuring immediately
after the bath therefore matters more in this case. Refining from 101 to
201 samples per stage changes all five cycle averages by less than 1.7e-7;
reported minima are sampled minima, not certified continuous-time bounds.

At a stationary cycle, the H stage still conserves energy, and the bath
returns to the cycle's starting energy. It can restore distant
correlations without further net cooling. Thus persistent within-cycle
correlation oscillations do not imply energy oscillations of the same size.

To check the small-period limit, keep tau_H/tau_B=80 and shorten both
stages:

## Bath-only evolution from an all-+x product state

This test changes only the initial state, to

\[
 \rho_x=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n|+\rangle\langle+|_i,
 \qquad |+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2.
\]

It keeps the edge-local Davies bath, b=6, kappa=2, sample normalization,
and the alternating 2x2-block images. There is no coherent-H stage and
no added Pauli noise. A circuit would prepare this initial state with
one Hadamard per system qubit. Initially <Sx_i>=1/2 and the purity is one,
while every distinct-pair Z correlation is zero. Thus the x polarization
does not itself supply the desired image Z correlations.

The exact eight-spin comparison uses the entire 65536-dimensional
operator space. The previous maximally mixed calculation could restrict
to the invariant zero magnetization-difference sector; this pure state
has coherences outside it. `ExactEdgeDynamics(sector='all')` retains
them. The default restricted mode still rejects unsupported input
coherences instead of silently deleting them. Both initial states are
evolved together by sparse exponential action of the same bath sum,
with no Trotter splitting or tensor truncation.

The main trajectory is sampled every 0.05 bath-time units, until both
states satisfy a full-state change bound below 1e-10 three consecutive
times. Both pass at t=5.95. A separate short run samples t=0 to 0.2 every
0.0025 to resolve the initial transient; it deliberately stops before
convergence and its `stabilized` flag is false. Output intervals are
sampling intervals, not Trotter steps.

|---|---:|---:|
| Initial full-H energy | 0 | -2.5 |
| Initial purity | 0.00390625 | 1 |
| Initial average Sx | 0 | 0.5 |
| Final sign-aligned far Gamma | 0.08064134102349 | 0.08064134102342 |
| Final full-H energy | -3.385032525832 | -3.385032525832 |

The final trace distance between the two states is 1.68e-13, and the
maximum final Z-profile difference over both seeds is 7.00e-14. Direct
one-step stationarity residuals are below 1.1e-11. The initially polarized
state's average Sx drops to 0.15014 at t=0.1 and 8.04e-6 at t=1.
The trace distance between the preparations drops from 0.99609375 at
t=0 to 0.118755 at t=0.2 and 3.30e-5 at t=1. Their Z profiles remain
close even during the transient: the largest sampled l-infinity
difference is 9.46e-4 at t=0.0575. Their local z means stay below 3.3e-16,
so raw and connected Z correlations agree within numerical accuracy.

There is a short heating transient from the all-+x state: its energy
rises from -2.5 to approximately -2.21650 at t=0.0475 before decreasing
to the common stationary value. This is a sampled peak. The edge bath
does not monotonically cool the full Hamiltonian for this initial state.
Starting with lower energy therefore does not lead to a lower final
energy or stronger distant correlations in this experiment.

full-state agreement. Larger runs monitor two correlation profiles,

the infinite-size limit.
